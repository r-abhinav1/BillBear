  • A bill with GST lines, a ₹ sign or an Indian address is in rupees, and nobody is asked.
  • Anything else needs one tap before the room opens. Without it, a ¥1,200 bill would quietly show as ₹1,200.

The leftover goes to whoever lost the most in rounding. When that's a tie, it goes to whoever joined the room first. Rupee bills work the same way, to the paisa.

Typing amounts

  • Yen, won and rupiah amounts are whole numbers. An amount like ¥12.50 is marked in red until it's fixed.
  • 12,50 is read as 12.50, the way it's written in Europe. 1,234 is still one thousand two hundred and thirty-four.
  • The bill total may differ by the bill's own rounding (up to ₹1, or 5 Rappen in Switzerland) without a warning.
  • The PDF writes the currency's code instead of its sign (JPY 2,574), because its fonts have no ¥ or ₹.
